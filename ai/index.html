Room 05

How I teach

Four principles I keep coming back to, whether the room is full of first-year students or working professionals.

First

Start with the problem

What problem does this concept solve?

I don't introduce a concept simply because it's next in the textbook. If a student can't say why a dictionary beats a list for this job, the syntax won't stick. So we feel the pain first, then meet the tool that removes it.

Second

Make students think

Predict, trace, debug, compare, explain.

Code completion tools can finish a line for you. They can't make you understand it. My exercises ask students to predict output before running, trace variables by hand, and explain why one version beats another.

Third

Learn through failure

Errors are information.

A traceback isn't a judgment. It's the computer telling you exactly where your mental model and reality disagree. Try one:
